### Question 1
**Question:** Least number divided by 7, 8, 11 always gives remainder 6?
*   **Options:** `[A) 624  B) 622  C) 616  D) 654]`
*   **Mental Trick:** LCM(7,8,11) = 616. Number = 616 + 6 = 622.
*   **Answer:** **B) 622**.

### Question 2
**Question:** GCD = 3, LCM = 36. One number = 9. Other?
*   **Options:** `[A) 18  B) 12  C) 14  D) 16]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Product = GCD × LCM = 3 × 36 = 108. Other = 108/9 = 12.
*   **Answer:** **B) 12**.

### Question 3
**Question:** Greatest number dividing 70 and 125 leaving remainders 5 and 8.
*   **Options:** `[A) 12  B) 11  C) 13  D) 15]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 70-5 = 65. 125-8 = 117. GCD(65, 117). 117 = 1×65 + 52. 65 = 1×52 + 13. 52 = 4×13. GCD = 13.
*   **Answer:** **C) 13**.

### Question 4 (i-powers)
**Question:** (i⁷⁹⁹ + i⁸⁰⁰ + i⁸⁰¹ + i⁸⁰²) / (i³⁴³ + i³⁴⁴ + i³⁴⁵ + i³⁴⁶)?
*   **Options:** `[A) 1  B) 2  C) 0  D) Can't determine]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Powers of i cycle every 4: i, -1, -i, 1. Any 4 consecutive powers sum to 0. Both numerator and denominator are 4 consecutive powers → both = 0. 0/0 is undefined.
*   **Answer:** **D) Can't be determined**.

### Question 6 (Perfect square factors)
**Question:** How many factors of 1080 are perfect squares?
*   **Options:** `[A) 4  B) 6  C) 8  D) 5]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 1080 = 2³ × 3³ × 5¹. For perfect square factors, each prime power must be even.
*   2: {0,2} → 2 choices. 3: {0,2} → 2. 5: {0} → 1. Total = 2×2×1 = 4.
*   **Answer:** **A) 4**.

### Question 7 (Odd factors)
**Question:** Factors of 2⁴ × 5³ × 7⁴ that are odd?
*   **Options:** `[A) 100  B) 99  C) 20  D) 24]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Odd factors → power of 2 must be 0. Count = (3+1)(4+1) = 4 × 5 = 20.
*   **Answer:** **C) 20**.

### Question 8 (Highest power)
**Question:** Highest power of 12 in 100!?
*   **Options:** `[A) 12  B) 48  C) 24  D) 97]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 12 = 2² × 3. Power of 2 in 100! = 50+25+12+6+3+1 = 97. Power of 2² = 97/2 = 48.
*   Power of 3 in 100! = 33+11+3+1 = 48. Limiting factor = min(48, 48) = 48.
*   **Answer:** **B) 48**.

### Question 9 (HCF/LCM)
**Question:** HCF of 2472, 1284, N is 12. LCM = 2³×3²×5×103×107. Find N.
*   **Options:** `[A) 2²×3²×5  B) 2²×3²×7  C) 2²×3²×103  D) None]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 2472 = 8×309 = 2³×3×103. 1284 = 4×321 = 2²×3×107. HCF = 12 = 2²×3.
*   N must have exactly 2² and 3 (at minimum for HCF=12, but LCM needs 3²). So N must have 3².
*   LCM has factor 5: neither 2472 nor 1284 has 5, so N must have 5. N = 2²×3²×5.
*   Verify: HCF(2³×3×103, 2²×3×107, 2²×3²×5) = 2²×3 = 12 ✅. LCM has all prime factors ✅.
*   **Answer:** **A) 2²×3²×5**.

### Question 10 (Remainder)
**Question:** Remainder of (12345678)⁷⁵ ÷ 9?
*   **Options:** `[A) 0  B) 1  C) 8  D) 5]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Sum of digits of 12345678 = 1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8 = 36. 36 mod 9 = 0. So 12345678 ≡ 0 (mod 9). Any power of 0 = 0.
*   **Answer:** **A) 0**.

### Question 11 (499th digit)
**Question:** (34128700)²⁴⁹. Find 499th digit.
*   **Options:** `[A) 7  B) 9  C) 3  D) 1]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 34128700 has 8 digits. (34128700)²⁴⁹ means repeating "34128700" 249 times = 1992 digits. 499th digit: 499/8 = 62 remainder 3. 3rd digit of "34128700" = 1.
*   Wait — this is the number written 249 times, not raised to power! The expression means concatenation.
*   499 = 62×8 + 3. 3rd digit = '1'.
*   **Answer:** **D) 1**.

### Question 12 (Trailing zeros)
**Question:** Start multiplying from 20 consecutively. How many numbers to get 3 trailing zeros?
*   **Options:** `[A) 11  B) 10  C) 6  D) 5]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Zeros come from pairs of 2×5. Count 5s: 20(1), 25(2), 30(1), 35(1) = 5 fives by 35.
*   20 to 25 = 6 numbers. Already 3 fives (20 has one, 25 has two). Plenty of 2s. So 6 numbers (20-25).
*   Wait: 20×21×22×23×24×25. Fives: 20=1, 25=2 → total 3. Twos: abundant. 3 trailing zeros ✅.
*   **Answer:** **C) 6**.

### Question 13 (Rightmost non-zero)
**Question:** Rightmost non-zero digit of 1430³⁴³ + 1470³⁶⁷?
*   **Options:** `[A) 1  B) 3  C) 7  D) 5]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 1430 ends in 0. Powers of 0 end in 0. 1430³⁴³ ends in ...000. 1470³⁶⁷ also ends in ...000.
*   Sum of two numbers ending in 0s. Need to go deeper: 1430 = 143 × 10. 1430³⁴³ = 143³⁴³ × 10³⁴³. Similarly 1470³⁶⁷ = 147³⁶⁷ × 10³⁶⁷. The first has 343 zeros, second 367. When added, last non-zero digit is determined by the number with fewer trailing zeros (343). So rightmost non-zero = last digit of 143³⁴³.
*   143 last digit = 3. Powers of 3 cycle: 3,9,7,1 (period 4). 343 mod 4 = 3. Last digit = 7.
*   **Answer:** **C) 7**.

**Question:** HCF of 9/10, 12/25, 18/35, 21/40?

### Question 16 (Remainder)
**Question:** Divided by 18 gives remainder 7. Same number divided by 12 gives remainder n. How many values of n?
*   **Options:** `[A) 2  B) 0  C) 1  D) 3]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Number = 18k + 7. Mod 12: (18k+7) mod 12 = (6k+7) mod 12. k=0: 7. k=1: 13 mod 12=1. k=2: 19 mod 12=7. Pattern cycles every 2: n ∈ {1, 7}. Two values.
*   **Answer:** **A) 2**.

### Question 18 (Coprime)
**Question:** abc = 9000. (a,b), (b,c), (c,a) are all coprime pairs. a+b+c?
*   **Options:** `[A) 142  B) 1009  C) 119  D) None]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 9000 = 2³×3²×5³. Since pairs are coprime, each prime goes entirely to one number.
*   Distribute: {2³=8, 3²=9, 5³=125}. a+b+c = 8+9+125 = 142.
*   **Answer:** **A) 142**.

### Question 19 (Perfect cube)
**Question:** A has 16 factors, B has 28, C has 30, D has 27. Which could be a perfect cube?
*   **Options:** `[A) A and B  B) B and C  C) A, B and C  D) B and D]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Perfect cube: all exponents in prime factorization are multiples of 3. Number of factors = product of (exponent+1). For a cube, each (exponent+1) ≡ 1 mod 3. So total factors = product of numbers ≡ 1 mod 3.
*   16: try 16=4×4 → each factor 4≡1 mod 3 ✅. 28: 28=4×7 → 7≡1 mod 3 ✅, 4≡1 ✅. 30: 30=2×3×5. 2≡2 mod 3 ✗. 27: 27=3³ → each 3≡0 mod 3 ✗. OR 27=27 (1 prime, exponent 26, 26 is not mult of 3). Hmm, let me reconsider: 27 = 27×1: single prime p²⁶. 26 mod 3 = 2 ✗. But 27=3×9=3×3×3: p²×q²×r² =(pqr)² not cube. 27=1×1×27: p²⁶ not cube. Actually try: can 27 factors come from a cube? (3a+1)(3b+1)... = 27. 4×4 = 16 ✗. 4×7=28 ✗. So: 27=27 needs 3a+1=27→a=26/3 ✗. 27=1×27 ✗. Not possible.
*   A(16) ✅, B(28) ✅. C and D cannot.
*   **Answer:** **A) A and B**.

### Question 20 (Max measure)

### Question 21 (3-digit abc)
**Question:** 3-digit number 'abc' has 3 factors. How many factors does 'abcabc' have?
*   **Options:** `[a) 16  b) 24  c) 16 or 24  d) 20]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 3 factors → abc = p² (prime squared). abcabc = abc × 1001 = p² × 7 × 11 × 13.
*   Factors = (2+1)(1+1)(1+1)(1+1) = 3×2×2×2 = 24. (Assuming p ≠ 7,11,13.)
*   If p = 7: abc = 49. abcabc = 7³×11×13. Factors = 4×2×2 = 16.
*   If p = 11: abc = 121. abcabc = 7×11³×13. Factors = 16. Same for p = 13 (169).
*   **Answer:** **c) 16 or 24**.

### Question 22 (Most divisors)
**Question:** Most divisors among 99, 101, 176, 182?
*   **Options:** `[a) 99  b) 101  c) 176  d) 182]`
*   **Mental Trick:** 99 = 9×11 = 3²×11 → 6 divisors. 101 = prime → 2. 176 = 16×11 = 2⁴×11 → 10. 182 = 2×91 = 2×7×13 → 8.
*   **Answer:** **c) 176**.
