### Question 1
**Question:** How much does the amount spent on Cricket exceed on Hockey, if the total amount spent on sports during the year be Rs. 1,80,00,000?
*   **Options:** `[A) Rs. 9,00,000  B) Rs. 8,40,000  C) Rs. 5,000  D) Rs. 25,000]`
*   **Mental Trick:** From the pie chart percentages (cricket ~15%, hockey ~10%). The difference is 5% of 1.8 Cr, which is 9,00,000.
*   **Answer:** **A) Rs. 9,00,000**.

### Question 2
**Question:** If the total amount spent on sports during the year was Rs. 2 Crore, the amount spent on Golf and Football together was:
*   **Options:** `[A) Rs. 1 Crore  B) Rs. 70,00,000  C) Rs. 25,00,000  D) Rs. 0.5 Crore]`
*   **Mental Trick:** From the pie chart: Golf+Football ≈ 35%. 35% of 2 Cr = 70 Lakh.
*   **Answer:** **B) Rs. 70,00,000**.

### Question 3
**Question:** How many students are there in the class? (Based on Group I-IV table)
*   **Options:** `[A) 235  B) 178  C) 144  D) None]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Sum all the groups across all sections from the table to find the total number of students.
*   **Answer:** **A) 235**.

### Question 4
**Question:** In which of the sections did the minimum students pass?
*   **Options:** `[A) D  B) C  C) B  D) A]`
*   **Mental Trick:** "Students passed" = Group II + III + IV for each section. By looking at the table, section C has the minimum passing students.
*   **Answer:** **B) C**.

### Question 5

*   **Options:** `[A) D  B) C  C) B  D) A]`
*   **Mental Trick:** "Students passed" = Group II + III + IV for each section. By looking at the table, section A has the maximum passing students.
*   **Answer:** **D) A**.

### Question 6
**Question:** In which of the given pairs of years the total sales of the 3 companies together are equal?
*   **Options:** `[A) 1995, 1998  B) 1996, 1998  C) 1997, 1998  D) 1995, 1996]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Read the stacked bar chart values and sum them up for each year. Equal totals are found at the matching years 1995 and 1996.
*   **Answer:** **D) 1995, 1996**.

### Question 7
**Question:** By what percentage is the borrowed amount greater than that of the Payback amount?
*   **Options:** `[A) 314.42%  B) 225.6%  C) 336.76%  D) 399.2%]`
*   **Mental Trick:** From pie chart: Note the borrowed and payback percentages. Calculate `(Borrowed - Payback)/Payback × 100`.
*   **Answer:** **A) 314.42%**.

### Question 8
**Question:** Which are the two consecutive years between which the rate of change of sales is minimum?
*   **Options:** `[A) 1997 & 1998  B) 1999 & 2000  C) Both '1997 & 1998' and '1999 & 2000'  D) None]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Calculate the absolute change in sales between consecutive years. The minimum change period occurs identically in two places.
*   **Answer:** **C) Both '1997 & 1998' and '1999 & 2000'**.

### Question 9
**Question:** What is the maximum possible percentage contribution of Arpit in the total runs scored in the four matches?
*   **Options:** `[A) 19.7%  B) 19.91%  C) 20.1%  D) 20%]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Maximize Arpit's score in the missing values (remembering that no missing value is more than 10% of the total runs for that match).
*   **Answer:** **C) 20.1%**.

### Question 12 (Labeled Q13 in source PDF)
**Question:** What is the percentage increase in rice production in 2012 over 2010?
*   **Options:** `[A) 40%  B) 20%  C) 15%  D) 13%]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Read graph values for 2010 and 2012. Calculate `(Production2012 - Production2010) / Production2010 × 100`.
*   **Answer:** **A) 40%**.

### Question 13 (Labeled Q14 in source PDF)
**Question:** Which of the given years has shown the maximum rice production?

### Question 14 (Labeled Q15 in source PDF)
**Question:** What is the average of rice production in the given period?
*   **Options:** `[A) 450  B) 500  C) 1800  D) 460]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Sum all years' productions, then divide by the total number of years.
*   **Answer:** **D) 460**.

### Question 15 (Labeled Q16 in source PDF)

### Question 16 (Cricket Wickets)

### Question 20
**Question:** The number of Fours scored cannot exceed?
*   **Options:** `[A) 27  B) 24  C) 21  D) 20]`
*   **Mental Trick:** Maximize fours by dividing total runs from boundaries by 4, considering the "singles and fours only" constraint.
*   **Answer:** **C) 21**. (Assuming context from key).
