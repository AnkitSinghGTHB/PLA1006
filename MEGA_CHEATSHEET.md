## 1. 🧠 The Golden Rules of Mental Math
*Master these calculation shortcuts before attempting any word problems. They will save you minutes per question.*

### ⚡ Simplifications & Multiplications
*   **SAW Method (Split & Distribute):** Split hard numbers into a clean base $\pm$ a small number.
    *   *Trigger:* Large numbers close to multiples of 10.
    *   *Example:* $69 \times 7 \rightarrow (70 - 1) \times 7 = 490 - 7 = 483$.
*   **Oz Method (Multiples of 5):** Use fractions instead of heavy multiplication.
    *   *Trigger:* Multiplying by $5, 25, 125$.
    *   $\times 5 \rightarrow$ multiply by 10, divide by 2.
    *   $\times 25 \rightarrow$ multiply by 100, divide by 4.
    *   $\times 125 \rightarrow$ multiply by 1000, divide by 8.

### 🟩 Squares & Cubes
*   **Minions Method (2-digit squares):** Based on $(a+b)^2$. Format mentally as $a^2 | b^2 + (2ab \times 10)$.
    *   *Example:* $32^2 \rightarrow 09|04 + (3 \times 2 \times 2) \times 10 \rightarrow 0904 + 120 = 1024$.
*   **Mirror Squares:** Memorize the reflections: $12^2 = 144 \leftrightarrow 21^2 = 441$. $13^2 = 169 \leftrightarrow 31^2 = 961$.
*   **Ending in 5:** $N5^2 = [N \times (N+1)] | 25$. (*Ex:* $45^2 = (4 \times 5)|25 = 2025$).

### 💯 Fractions to Percentages (Memorize!)
*   $1/2 = 50\%$
*   $1/3 = 33.33\%$
*   $1/4 = 25\%$
*   $1/5 = 20\%$
*   $1/6 = 16.66\%$
*   *Application:* If asked for 16.66% of 216, just do $216 / 6 = 36$. No decimals needed!

## 2. 🧮 Quantitative Aptitude (Number System, Arithmetic, Mensuration)

### Number System & LCM/HCF
*   **When you see:** "Traffic lights changing simultaneously" or "Bells tolling together"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **LCM**. You are looking for the *next* common future event.
*   **When you see:** "Maximum length of a measuring rod" or "Dividing students into largest possible equal groups"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **HCF (GCD)**. You are looking for the largest common divisor.
*   **When you see:** "Find the unit digit of $3^{145}$"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Cyclicity Rule**. Divide exponent by 4. $145/4 \rightarrow$ remainder 1. So it acts like $3^1 = 3$.
*   **When you see:** "How many trailing zeros in $100!$?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Divide and Sum quotients by 5**. $100/5 = 20$. $20/5 = 4$. Total $= 24$ zeros.
*   **When you see:** "Find the 499th digit of $(34128700)^{249}$"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Trailing Zeros Logic**. The number ends in $(100)^{249} \rightarrow 498$ zeros. The 499th digit from right is just the unit digit of $341287^{249}$.

### Percentages, Profit/Loss & Mixtures
*   **When you see:** "A shopkeeper uses a 900g weight instead of 1kg..."
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **False Weight Formula**. $Profit\% = \frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}} \times 100$. ($100/900 \times 100 = 11.11\%$).
*   **When you see:** "A trader gives a 20% discount but still makes a 20% profit. Ratio of MP to CP?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **MP/CP Ratio Formula**. $\frac{MP}{CP} = \frac{100 + P\%}{100 - D\%}$. ($120/80 = 3/2$).
*   **When you see:** "Successive price changes (e.g., +20%, then -10%)"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Net Change Formula**. $A + B + \frac{A \times B}{100}$. ($20 - 10 + \frac{-200}{100} = +8\%$).
*   **When you see:** Liquid being repeatedly removed and replaced (e.g., milk replaced with water).
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Mixture Replacement Formula**. $Final = Initial \times (1 - \frac{\text{Replacement Vol}}{\text{Total Vol}})^n$.

### Simple & Compound Interest
*   **When you see:** "Find the difference between CI and SI for 2 years"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **2-Year Diff Formula**. $Diff = P \times (\frac{R}{100})^2$.
*   **When you see:** "Find the difference between CI and SI for 3 years"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **3-Year Diff Formula**. $Diff = P \times (\frac{R}{100})^2 \times (3 + \frac{R}{100})$.
*   **When you see:** "Calculate 2-year CI without long formulas"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Effective CI%**. $2R + \frac{R^2}{100}$. (For 5%, effective rate = 10.25%).

### Averages, Ages & Partnerships
*   **When you see:** "Average of 5 large numbers: 82, 85, 87, 89, 92"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **The Deviation Method**. Guess base 85. Deviations: -3, 0, +2, +4, +7. Sum = +10. Avg dev = +2. New avg = $85 + 2 = 87$.
*   **When you see:** "Teacher joins the class, average goes up by 1kg"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Inclusion Formula**. $New Person = Old Avg + (\text{Total New People} \times \text{Change})$.
*   **When you see:** "10 years ago, A was half B. Today ratio is 3:4"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Constant Age Difference Trick**. Age differences never change. Make the gap between ratios equal, then compare the unit jump to real years.
*   **When you see:** Partnership investments with different timeframes.
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Golden Partnership Ratio**. Profit Ratio = $(I_1 \times T_1) : (I_2 \times T_2)$.

### Geometry & Mensuration
*   **When you see:** Finding the volume/area of a Cylinder, Cone, or Sphere.
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Target Pi (11) Trick**. Check the options! The answer is almost always a multiple of 11. (Alternating sum of digits difference must be 0 or 11).
*   **When you see:** "A 3D object is melted and recast into N smaller shapes"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Volume Constancy**. $Volume_{big} = N \times Volume_{small}$. Ignore surface area entirely!

## 3. ⏳ Time, Speed, Distance & Work

### Time & Work
*   **When you see:** "A does it in 10 days, B in 15 days. Time together?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **The LCM Method**. Never use fractions like $1/x + 1/y$. Total Work = LCM(10, 15) = 30. Efficiencies: A=3, B=2. Total Time = $30 / 5 = 6$ days.
*   **When you see:** "A and B work, wage is \$500"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Wage Division Rule**. Divide wages in the ratio of **Work Done (Efficiency)**, NOT days taken.
*   **When you see:** "15 men build 10m wall in 5 days, how many for 20m in 3 days?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **MDH Equivalence Rule**. $\frac{M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2}{W_2}$.

### Time, Speed, Distance (TSD)
*   **When you see:** "A takes 1 hr longer than B for the same distance"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Proportionality Rule**. If Distance is constant, Speed and Time are inversely proportional. (Speed Ratio $4:5 \rightarrow$ Time Ratio $5:4$).
*   **When you see:** "Goes at 60kmph, returns at 40kmph. Average speed?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Avg Speed Formula**. $\frac{2 \times S_1 \times S_2}{S_1 + S_2}$. (Not 50! It's 48kmph).
*   **When you see:** "If I walk 4kmph I am 10 mins late, 5kmph I am 5 mins early"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Late/Early Formula**. $Distance = \frac{S_1 \times S_2}{|S_1 - S_2|} \times \text{Time Difference (in hours)}$.

### Trains, Boats, Races & Escalators
*   **When you see:** "Train crosses a platform/bridge"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** Total Distance = $Length_{Train} + Length_{Platform}$. Convert kmph to m/s by multiplying by $5/18$.
*   **When you see:** "A gives B a headstart of 10m in a 100m race"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** This just means A runs 100m, while B only needs to run 90m in the exact same timeframe.
*   **When you see:** People moving on a moving Escalator.
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Boats & Streams Logic**. Downstream (With escalator) = $S_1 + S_2$. Upstream (Against) = $S_1 - S_2$.

## 4. 🧩 Logical & Analytical Reasoning

### Clocks & Calendars
*   **When you see:** "Angle between hands at 4:20"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Golden Clock Formula**. $Angle = |30H - 5.5M|$. ($|120 - 110| = 10^\circ$).
*   **When you see:** "When will hands coincide?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** Same formula, set $Angle = 0$.
*   **When you see:** "How many times do hands overlap in a day?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Memory Stats**. Overlap ($0^\circ$) = 22 times. Opposite ($180^\circ$) = 22 times. Right Angle ($90^\circ$) = 44 times.
*   **When you see:** "What day will it be after 65 days?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Odd Days Concept**. Divide by 7. $65/7 \rightarrow$ Remainder is 2. Just add 2 days to the current day.
*   **When you see:** "When will the calendar of 2024 repeat?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Repetition Rule**. Leap Year = +28 yrs. Leap+1 = +6 yrs. Leap+2/+3 = +11 yrs.

### Blood Relations & Directions
*   **When you see:** "Pointing to a photograph, A said..."
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** Start at the word **"MY"** in the sentence and resolve the family tree backwards step-by-step.
*   **When you see:** Complex walking patterns (e.g., Up 3, Right 4).
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Pythagorean Triplets**. 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17. Never calculate square roots manually if you spot these.

### Puzzles & Data Arrangements
*   **When you see:** "Circular seating with 8 people"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Center Anchor**. Face center: Left = Clockwise. Face out: Left = Anti-Clockwise. Start plotting from the bottom (6 o'clock) to visualize easily.
*   **When you see:** Matching people to cities or professions.
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Tick/Cross Matrix**. Draw a grid. The moment you place one $\checkmark$, fill that entire row and column with $\times$'s.

## 5. 📊 Data Interpretation & Sufficiency

### Data Interpretation (DI)
*   **When you see:** Options that are widely spaced (e.g., 40%, 75%, 105%)
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Heavy Rounding**. NEVER calculate exact values. Mentally round $49,732$ to $50k$.
*   **When you see:** A Pie Chart and a Table together (Multiple Charts).
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Ratio Only Strategy**. If the question asks for a percentage or ratio, ignore the absolute total population entirely. Calculate using only the degree/percentage units.
*   **When you see:** "What % of a Pie Chart is a 72 degree sector?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Divide by 3.6**. $72 / 3.6 = 20\%$.

### Data Sufficiency (DS)
*   **When you see:** "What is the exact price of an apple, banana, and cherry?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **Variable Counting Trick**. 3 variables requires 3 unique equations. If the statements only provide 2 equations total, immediately mark "Insufficient". Saves 3 minutes of algebra.
*   **When you see:** "Is $X > Y$?"
    *   **$\rightarrow$ Use:** **The Yes/No Trap**. If a statement gives you definitive proof that the answer is "NO", that statement **IS SUFFICIENT**. Sufficiency means having enough data to answer definitively, not that the answer must be positive.
