# Profit and Loss - Mental Math Solutions

[⬅️ Back to Main Navigation](./README.md) | [📖 Read Cheatsheet](./01_Arithmetic_Percentages_CheatSheet.md)

This document contains step-by-step mental math strategies for the `Profit Loss.pdf` practice set.

> [!TIP]
> **The Base 100 Rule:** When dealing with successive markups, discounts, or dishonest weights, always assume the standard Cost Price (CP) is 100 or 1000g, and chain multipliers instead of subtracting fractions.

---

### Question 1
**Question:** A necklace and a shop were sold for 1 lakh each. One sale resulted in 20% loss, the other 20% profit. Net profit/loss?
*   **Options:** `[A) 5.26% B) 4% C) 6.20% D) 7.12%]`
*   **Mental Trick (The $(x/10)^2$ Formula):**
    *   Whenever two items are SOLD for the exact same price (`SP1 = SP2`), and one has an `x%` gain while the other has an `x%` loss, there is **ALWAYS a net loss**.
    *   Formula: `Loss % = (x/10)^2`.
    *   Here, $x = 20$. So, $(20/10)^2 = 2^2 = $ **4%**.
*   **Answer:** **B) 4%**.

### Question 2
**Question:** If chocolates are bought at 10 for a rupee, how many must be sold for a rupee to gain a profit of 25%?
*   **Options:** `[A) 16  B) 8  C) 4  D) 2]`
*   **Mental Trick (Inverse Correlation):**
    *   To gain a 25% profit, you need to sell the goods at `125%` (or `5/4`) of their value. 
    *   Since the price is fixed at "one rupee", the **quantity metric inversely drops** to reflect that profit.
    *   Multiply original quantity by the inverse: `10 * (4/5)` = **8 chocolates**.
*   **Answer:** **B) 8**.

### Question 3
**Question:** A dishonest vendor uses a 1350g weight instead of 1kg while buying, and a 900g weight instead of 1kg while selling. He claims to sell at Cost Price. Overall profit %?
*   **Options:** `[A) 30%  B) 60%  C) 50%  D) 40%]`
*   **Mental Trick:**
    *   He steals 350g per kg from the wholesaler (gets 1350g).
    *   He shorts the customer by 100g per kg (gives 900g).
    *   So, every time he sells 900g of material (his actual cost), he gets paid as if it were 1kg, but he originally bought 1350g for the price of 1kg.
    *   Net transaction: He sells 900g and gets the same money he spent on 1350g.
    *   Profit Margin = `(Goods remaining) / (Goods sold)`.
    *   He saved `1350 - 900 = 450g`. 
    *   Profit % = `450 / 900 = 1/2 = 50%`.
*   **Answer:** **C) 50%**.

### Question 4
**Question:** A shop owner marks up by 50%, then discounts by 10%. He uses false weights to reduce quantity sold by 20%. Net profit percentage?
*   **Options:** `[A) 57.5%  B) 68.75%  C) 52.5%  D) 41%]`
*   **Mental Trick (Successive Chaining):** Start with 100.
    1.  Markup 50% -> `100 * 1.5 = 150`.
    2.  Discount 10% -> `150 - 15 = 135`. (This is his price multiplier).
    3.  False weights: He reduces quantity by 20%, meaning he gives 80g instead of 100g. 
        *   His cost drops from 100 to 80, but he charges 135.
    *   Final Profit = `(SP - CP) / CP` = `(135 - 80) / 80` = `55 / 80`.
    *   Mental division: `55/80 = 11/16`. Since `1/16 = 6.25%`, `11 * 6.25 = 68.75%`.
*   **Answer:** **B) 68.75%**.

### Question 5
**Question:** A storekeeper allows a discount of 70% and still gains 15%. Find the marked price of an article costing Rs. 6000.
*   **Options:** `[A) 23000  B) 24000  C) 20000  D) 18000]`
*   **Mental Trick (The Golden Ratio):**
    *   Formula: `MP / CP = (100 + Profit%) / (100 - Discount%)`.
    *   `MP / 6000 = (100 + 15) / (100 - 70)`
    *   `MP / 6000 = 115 / 30`.
    *   `MP = 115 * 200 = 23000`.
*   **Answer:** **A) 23000**.

### Question 7
**Question:** What is the maximum percentage discount a merchant can offer to end up at no profit/loss if she initially marks up by 50%?
*   **Options:** `[A) 50%  B) 20%  C) 25%  D) 33.33%]`
*   **Mental Trick:**
    *   CP = 100. 
    *   Marked Up Price = 150.
    *   Needs to sell at 100 (no profit/loss). This requires a 50 Rupee drop.
    *   Discount % is based on the *Marked Price*, not the combined base!
    *   `Discount % = (Drop / MP) = 50 / 150 = 1/3 = 33.33%`.
*   **Answer:** **D) 33.33%**.

### Question 9
**Question:** The cost price of 40 books is same as selling price of 35 books. Profit or loss %?
*   **Options:** `[A) 12.5% loss  B) 14.5% profit  C) 14.2% profit  D) 12% loss]`
*   **Mental Trick (Quantity Rule):** 
    *   If `CP of X = SP of Y`, then `Profit/Loss % = (X - Y) / Y * 100`.
    *   `(40 - 35) / 35 = 5 / 35 = 1/7`.
    *   `1/7 = 14.28%`.
*   **Answer:** **C) 14.2% profit**.
